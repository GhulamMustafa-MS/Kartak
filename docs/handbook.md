# The handbook: syllabus and facts

**The one home for what the exam is based on.** The real exam is built on the official handbook
"Health Education Programme for Workers in Food Establishments", English edition, code
BK-FS-Workers-E-2025-01 (128 pages, Examination and Evaluation Center of Health Education Programme, Ministry of
Municipalities and Housing). The user shared it on 26 Oct 2026; it is kept **outside the repo** (it is
copyrighted and forbids copying, adapting or translating it). Every question in `data/questions.json` must agree
with the facts below (D22). Kartak's questions are written in its own words; never copy the handbook's
sentences or its "Test Your Knowledge" questions (D6).

## 1. Chapters (syllabus)

| Ch | Handbook chapter | Topics the questions cover |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction for You | Professionalism; health education (new, current, supervisors; refresher every 3 years); official vs self health inspection (everyone does self-inspection); who is a food handler; health certificate (medical check, vaccinations, valid 1 year, renew before expiry); training by an accredited centre; what to do when ill or injured |
| 2 | Introduction to Food Safety | Why food safety matters (200+ diseases spread by food, 75% of new diseases come from animals); why it matters more now; roles of government, industry, consumers and workers; benefits and costs of a food safety system; risks (pests, waste, disease); contamination at any stage of the food chain; hazard types (microbial, chemical, physical, allergy); direct vs cross-contamination |
| 3 | Microbial Food Contamination | Bacteria, viruses, moulds, parasites; good, spoilage and disease bacteria; sources (people, raw food, water, air/dust, pests, soil, waste); growth needs (food, moisture, time, warmth); temperatures; spores (Bacillus cereus in rice, Clostridium perfringens); toxins (Staphylococcus aureus survives cooking, Clostridium botulinum); controlling bacteria; low-risk, raw and high-risk foods |
| 4 | Chemical and Physical Food Contamination | Chemical: sources (cleaning residues, additives, unsafe containers, heavy metals, re-used frying oil, pesticides), accidental vs deliberate, control. Physical: hair, jewellery, metal, bones, dust, paint, plastic, pest remains; control and reporting |
| 5 | Food Spoilage | Signs (colour, smell, texture, taste); perishable, semi-perishable, non-perishable foods; causes (microbes, pests, freezer burn, absorbed chemicals, poor temperature and humidity control); control; swollen cans |
| 6 | Health Risks of Unsafe Food | Unsafe vs safe food; food poisoning (symptoms, 2-36 h incubation, outbreaks, who is most at risk); common bacteria (Staph aureus, C. perfringens, Salmonella, C. botulinum, B. cereus) and how to control each; foodborne diseases (Campylobacter, E. coli, Listeria, Shigella, Norovirus); food allergy vs intolerance, anaphylaxis, the 14 allergens; non-disease harm (stones, bone, metal) |
| 7 | Personal Hygiene for Food Handlers | How bacteria pass from people to food; hygiene at home, before work, before starting; clothing, head and beard cover, no jewellery or watch, work shoes, remove work clothes when leaving; when and how to wash hands; gloves; masks, hair and arm covers; wounds (blue waterproof plaster); sneezing; no eating, drinking or smoking; bad habits |
| 8 | Cleaning in Food Establishments | Cleaning vs disinfecting; food-contact, non-food-contact, hand-contact surfaces and cleaning tools; clean as you go; the six cleaning stages; three-sink method; wrong practices; waste and ideal bins; pests (types, risks, 8 signs, practices that attract them) |
| 9 | General Requirements for Food Establishments | Workflow from receiving to service in one direction; clean and unclean areas separated; water-resistant, easy-to-clean floors, walls, ceilings; safe tool materials; ventilation; lighting; hand-wash sinks with warm water and soap; separate sinks for food and for utensils; reporting faults |
| 10 | Food Safety Management System | Food safety management; HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points: prevents problems, 7 principles, 12 steps); prerequisite programmes (design, hygiene, cleaning, waste, pests, storage, training, complaints, traceability and recall, visitors); the 12 steps in order; records |
| 11 | Safe Food Handling | Time and temperature control; purchasing and transport; receiving and storage; preparation and cooking; holding, serving and display; what to do when something is unsafe (report, refuse, isolate, ask the supervisor) |
| 12 | Practical Skills | Hand washing; putting on and taking off gloves; measuring food temperature; receiving food; stock rotation (FIFO, best before vs use by); dry storage; fridge and freezer storage; washing and disinfecting vegetables; thawing; cleaning surfaces (6-stage and 4-stage methods); cleaning utensils (three-sink and two-sink methods) |
| 13 | Occupational Health and Safety | Causes of accidents; burns, cuts, slips and trips (about 35% of injuries), lifting, electric shock, chemicals, fire; prevention and first aid; personal protective equipment; first aid kit |

## 2. Facts and numbers (Kartak uses these; D22)

| Topic | Handbook fact |
|---|---|
| Danger zone | Bacteria grow fast between **5°C and 63°C**; best growth about **37°C** (body temperature); below 5°C very slow; above 63°C growth stops; **75°C and above** kills most harmful bacteria |
| Bacteria growth | Divide every **10-20 minutes**; one can become over **130,000 in 6 hours** |
| Freezing | Freezing (−12°C to −22°C) does not kill bacteria, it only stops them |
| Cooking | Centre of the food at least **75°C for 30 seconds** |
| Cooling | Cool hot food quickly, **within 1.5 hours** (the handbook's test answer); chapter 6 also says within 2 hours to below 5°C, so questions never offer both 1.5 and 2 hours as competing options |
| Reheating | Above **82°C** (rice at least 82°C). Reheating to only 60°C is wrong |
| Hot holding / display | **63°C or above** |
| Cold display | **8°C or below** |
| Fridge | Keep chilled food at **5°C or below**; set and check the fridge at **0-4°C** (also "4°C or below" for storage); eggs in the fridge at 5°C or below |
| Freezer | **−18°C or colder** (store and transport) |
| Transport | Chilled below 5°C; frozen below −18°C; check the vehicle is clean; load and unload quickly |
| Dry store | Below **25°C**, dry, ventilated; shelves at least **15 cm** above the floor; never on the floor |
| Thawing | In the **fridge at 0-4°C**, covered, or in the **microwave** on defrost; never at room temperature; check the packet (some foods cook from frozen) |
| Probe thermometer | Clean and disinfect before and after use; push into the thickest part (**2.5 cm**), not touching bone, sides or bottom; wait **15-20 seconds**; soups: stir, centre, at least **5 cm** deep; frozen food: between two packs, **30-45 seconds**; infrared thermometers read the surface only; record readings |
| Hand washing | At least **20 seconds**, **warm water** and liquid soap, at the hand-wash sink only. Steps: wet, soap, rub palms, between and around fingers, backs of hands, rinse, dry with paper towel, bin the towel (turn the tap off with the towel) |
| When to wash hands | Arriving at work; before handling food and utensils; before putting on gloves and between changes; before and after raw food; after the toilet; after touching body or clothes; after breaks; after sneezing or coughing; after rubbish; after chemicals |
| Gloves | Wash hands before; take from the box by the edge; don't touch the outside; change **every 30 minutes**, after rubbish or breaks, when torn or dirty; never re-use; take off inside out; wash hands after; gloves don't replace hand washing; only for food |
| Clothing | Clean every day, washable, light-coloured, few pockets, covers own clothes; head and beard covered; no jewellery or watch; washable work shoes; take work clothes off when leaving (smoking, toilet); mask over nose and mouth, change when wet or after breaks |
| Illness | Don't come to work with fever, diarrhoea, vomiting, sore throat with fever, jaundice (yellow skin or eyes), infected wounds or skin infections; tell the supervisor, stop handling food, see a doctor |
| Wounds | Stop work, cover with a **waterproof, coloured (blue)**, secure plaster, wear a glove over it; report if a plaster falls into food |
| Sneezing | Move away from food; if you can't, cover with your arm; wash hands after |
| Not allowed | Eating, drinking or smoking in food areas; touching hair, face or beard; licking fingers; blowing into bags; personal things or outdoor clothes in food areas; opening bins by hand; hand-wash sink for food or tools; food sink for hands or tools |
| Health certificate | Needs medical checks and vaccinations; **valid 1 year**; renew before it expires. Refresher health education **every 3 years** |
| Food poisoning | Symptoms (stomach pain, vomiting, nausea, diarrhoea, fever) usually **2-36 hours** after eating; last a day to a week. Most at risk: elderly, **children under 5**, seriously ill, pregnant women, food handlers working with raw food |
| Allergens (14) | Gluten grains (wheat, barley, oats, rye), crustaceans (shrimp, crab, lobster), fish, peanuts, tree nuts, milk, sulphites (10 ppm or more), celery, eggs, mustard, sesame, molluscs (squid, mussels), lupin, soya. Symptoms within minutes or hours; anaphylaxis can kill even from a tiny amount |
| Cans | Don't use swollen, leaking, dented-seam or holed cans (botulism risk); report to the supervisor |
| Cleaning | Cleaning removes visible dirt (hot water + detergent); disinfecting kills germs (sanitiser or very hot water). Six stages: initial clean, main clean (hot water about **50°C** + detergent), first rinse, disinfect (chemical or **82°C** water), final rinse if needed, dry. Food-contact surfaces after every use |
| 4-stage surface method | Initial clean, spray sanitiser and scrub, leave for the contact time (usually **30 seconds to 5 minutes**), air dry or paper towel; no rinse unless the label says |
| Three-sink method | Scrape into the bin; sink 1 wash at about 50°C with detergent; sink 2 rinse; sink 3 disinfect (**75°C for 30 s or 82°C for 10 s**, or chemical); air dry upside down on racks; never dry with cloth towels; one direction only. Two-sink method: wash and rinse in sink 1, disinfect in sink 2 |
| Vegetables | Wash under cold running water (brush), soak in disinfectant at the right strength and time, rinse under cold running water |
| Stock | FIFO: older stock in front, new behind or underneath; label received and expiry dates; "best before" on long-life foods (cans, dried, frozen); "use by" on high-risk perishables (meat, fish, dairy); throw away expired food |
| Waste | Bins away from food, in good condition, covered; ideal bin: pest-proof, **foot-operated**, bag and tight lid; empty before full |
| Pests | Cockroaches, flies, ants, rats, mice, birds, storage beetles, cats and dogs. Signs: larvae or cocoons, dead pests, droppings, live pests, odd smells, scratching sounds at night, grease marks, gnaw marks. Report to the supervisor; never use pesticide yourself; food covered before any pest treatment |
| Building | Work flows one way from receiving to service; clean and dirty areas separated; floors, walls, ceilings water-resistant and easy to clean; good ventilation and lighting; hand-wash sinks with warm water and soap; separate sinks for food and for utensils; report faults (broken lights, cracks) |
| HACCP | Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points; prevents problems before they happen; 7 principles, 12 steps: team, describe product, intended use, flow diagram, check the diagram on site, hazard analysis, critical control points, critical limits, monitoring, corrective actions, verification, records |
| Safety at work | Slips and trips about **35%** of injuries; burns: cool under cold water **10-20 minutes**; chemicals in eyes: rinse **15-20 minutes** with eyelids open; cut away from your body; pan handles away from flames; report every injury |

## 3. What changed in Kartak because of this (28 Oct 2026, D22)

The first bank used general WHO numbers (danger zone 5-60°C, cook and reheat to 70°C, hot holding 60°C,
cold running water for thawing). These were replaced everywhere by the handbook's numbers above.
